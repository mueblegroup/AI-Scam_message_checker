Scam awareness guide

Recognise the hook before it becomes a loss.

Most scam messages try to make you react quickly. Learn the common patterns, slow the conversation down, and verify every unexpected request through an independent channel.

01

Links and account alerts

Unexpected links are often used to steal login details or payment information.

  • “Verify your account” or “payment failed”
  • Shortened URLs or misspelled brand domains
  • Threats that an account will be suspended
  • Requests to install an APK or remote-access app
02

Reply and conversation hooks

A simple reply can confirm that your number is active and open a social-engineering conversation.

  • “Reply YES/NO,” “if keen,” or “text back”
  • Wrong-number messages that become overly friendly
  • Unexpected loan, job or investment introductions
  • Requests to move the chat to another platform
03

Money and reward pressure

Scammers use fear, opportunity or small fees to make the first payment feel reasonable.

  • Parcel, customs, toll or redelivery fees
  • Guaranteed returns and easy task income
  • Prizes, refunds and free gift claims
  • Upfront deposits or processing charges
04

Impersonation and secrets

Logos and organisation names can be copied. Verify the person or institution separately.

  • Bank, police, tax authority or courier claims
  • A boss or family member using a “new number”
  • Requests for OTP, TAC, PIN, CVV or passwords
  • Instructions not to tell anyone about the request
✓

What to do when a message feels suspicious

PauseDo not click, reply, install anything or transfer money while pressured.
Verify independentlyOpen the official app yourself or use a phone number from the organisation's real website.
Block and reportSave evidence, block the sender and report the message through your platform or relevant authority.
Remember: Legitimate banks and services should never ask you to reveal an OTP, password, TAC, PIN or CVV through an unsolicited message.
